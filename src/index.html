Refiner distillate wholesale × 42 − refiner crude acquisition cost

Both legs are refiner survey averages rather than spot markers: the product leg is the US average wholesale/resale price refiners charged for No. 2 distillate, and the crude leg is their composite acquisition cost, which lags the market and blends domestic with imported barrels. The level therefore sits above the spot crack and moves more slowly. EIA discontinued the refiner product series in 2022-03; the crude series continues.

Every refresh is compared against the previous one, and any change to an already-published value is logged. Spot prices are rarely restated; the monthly refiner series are revised routinely as survey responses come in, so that is where most entries come from. New observations appearing for the first time are not revisions and are not listed. Comparison is skipped when the upstream source changes, since EIA and the FRED fallback carry slightly different observation counts.

The formula

Diesel crack is ULSD minus crude, per barrel. ULSD quotes in $/gal, so multiply by 42:

crack ($/bbl) = ULSD ($/gal) × 42 − WTI ($/bbl)
crack ($/bbl) = ULSD ($/gal) × 42 − Brent ($/bbl)  ← European / Atlantic-basin

Use Brent instead of WTI if you're looking at European / Atlantic-basin refining margins. There's no separate "diesel contract" — NY Harbor ULSD (NYMEX ticker HO) is the diesel benchmark; the name is a leftover from when it was heating oil.

Notes on the series

  • Depth. The distillate leg is spliced: ULSD (DDFUELNYH) from 2006-06-14 onward, No. 2 heating oil (DHOILNYH) before that. Same NY Harbor barge market, different sulfur spec — ULSD carries a small quality premium over high-sulfur heating oil, so pre-2006 levels are marginally understated. That splice is what buys the history: daily ULSD alone only reaches 2006. Forty years, not fifty — 1986-06-02 is where the daily spot record begins.
  • "European" crack. This is NY Harbor distillate against Brent — the Atlantic-basin margin as the formula defines it, not a Rotterdam gasoil quote. A true northwest-European crack would use ICE Low Sulphur Gasoil against Brent; that series isn't in a free keyless feed.
  • Spot, not futures. These are physical spot assessments, so the level differs slightly from the front-month HO1!−CL1! futures crack.
  • Refinery economics. If you're modelling a whole refinery rather than the diesel leg, switch on the 3-2-1 series — three barrels of crude in, two of gasoline and one of distillate out, computed as (2 × gasoline × 42 + distillate × 42 − 3 × WTI) ÷ 3 from NY Harbor legs. The Gulf Coast series is usually the better regional reference than NY Harbor for US refining margins; both are off by default.
  • How far back diesel actually goes. Fifty years isn't reachable on the diesel leg from public data at any resolution. Daily spot begins 1986-06-02. The deepest published US distillate price of any kind is the refiner wholesale series at 1983-01 — that's the monthly panel, and it's a different price basis, so it's shown separately rather than pretending to extend the spot line. The crude side goes back to 1974 either way; diesel is the binding constraint.
